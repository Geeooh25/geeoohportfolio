The mind behind the work

Geeooh's workspace.
01

I noticed most software feels like it was built by people who don't care.

The apps we use every day — the ones that freeze, the forms that lose your data, the interfaces that make you feel stupid — they weren't designed to be bad. They just weren't designed to be good. Someone shipped them and moved on. I decided I never wanted to be that person.

02

So I became obsessive about the details nobody notices.

The 200ms delay that subconsciously frustrates users. The button label that's technically correct but emotionally wrong. The error message that blames the user instead of guiding them. These things matter. Not because users will thank you — they won't notice. But they'll feel the difference. And feeling is everything.

03

The question I ask before every project: "What will they feel when they leave?"

Not "what stack should I use?" Not "how fast can I ship?" Those are important, but they're secondary. The primary question is emotional. Because people don't remember what technology you used. They remember how your work made them feel.

04

This portfolio isn't a resume. It's an invitation.

If you're building something and you want it to feel like it was made by someone who genuinely gave a damn — you're in the right place. I work with founders, teams, and dreamers who believe that software should make people feel something. Let's build that.

The details you don't notice are the ones that matter most.
